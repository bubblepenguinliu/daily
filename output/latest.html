Genie 试图解决的问题

问题始于工程团队之间如何相互支持。

在大型软件驱动型公司中，许多团队构建平台，供其他开发者用来完成工作。平台可能提供用于软件部署、数据处理或 ML 工作流等活动的工具。使用这些平台的其他团队的开发者不可避免地需要帮助，以解决配置、错误或理解不熟悉的功能。

延伸这种“平台团队-产品团队”的支持模式在 Google、Meta 等公司普遍存在，通常用内部工单系统或 Slack 频道处理，但缺乏自动化会导致重复问题消耗大量人力。

在典型设置中，负责管理平台的团队会指派一名工程师在特定时间段内处理传入的问题。这项职责通常涉及提供 on-call 支持。虽然 on-call 工作确实可能涉及紧急生产事故，但 Genie 试图解决的问题针对的是平台团队开发者通过 Slack 频道提供的日常技术支持。

例如，产品团队的开发者可能询问如何配置平台功能。值班工程师阅读问题并要求更多细节。提问者回复，然后再次等待。最终，值班工程师找到相关文档或解释所需步骤。即使是一个相对简单的问题，也可能涉及多轮来回对话和等待。

数据Uber 内部数据显示，支持频道每月约 45K 个问题，平均每个问题需要 2-3 轮交互，按工程师时薪估算，每年浪费数千小时。

Uber 报告称，其内部支持频道每月收到约 45K 个问题。在这种数量下，重复问题消耗了大量工程时间，而平台团队本可以将这些时间用于更好的事情。

回答这些问题所需的大部分信息已经存在于某处。然而，它通常分散在 Engwiki（Uber 的内部工程 wiki）、内部 Stack Overflow 和其他文档中。找到正确的信息变得如此困难，以至于向另一个人提问要容易得多。

实操你可以用开源工具如 LlamaIndex 或 LangChain 搭建类似原型：将内部文档向量化存入 ChromaDB，用 GPT-4 或本地模型做 RAG，再接入 Slack bot 测试效果。

Genie 旨在通过开发者已经寻求帮助的 Slack 频道，让现有知识变得可用。

Genie 做什么？

Genie 将查找信息与解释信息相结合。

大型语言模型（LLM）可以生成可读的解释并回答技术问题。然而，其通用训练并不会自动赋予它关于 Uber 私有平台、内部流程或文档的知识。

背景LLM 的知识截止于训练数据，且不包含企业私有数据。RAG 通过外挂知识库解决此问题，无需重新训练模型，是当前企业落地 LLM 的主流方案。

因此，Uber 需要一种将语言模型连接到其内部知识库的方法。有两种方法有助于实现这一点：微调（fine-tuning）和检索增强生成（RAG）。

微调意味着使用为特定目的选择的示例进一步训练现有模型。Uber 解释说，这种方法需要收集高质量、多样化的训练示例，以及足够的计算资源，以便在新示例出现时更新基础模型。

质疑微调的成本常被低估：一次全参数微调需数十张 GPU 数小时，且新文档出现后需重新训练。RAG 则只需更新向量库，成本低得多，但检索质量依赖嵌入模型和分块策略。

另一方面，RAG 在提问时提供相关信息。系统首先搜索知识集合，检索有用的段落，然后将这些段落连同问题一起交给 LLM。模型利用所有这些信息生成答案。

例如，假设开发者询问如何更改内部平台中的某个设置。RAG 系统搜索平台文档，找到解释该设置的段落，并将其交给模型。模型将检索到的信息转化为适合该问题的回答。

Uber 为 Genie 选择 RAG，因为它可以从现有文档开始，更快准备上线，而无需先组装微调数据集。这仍然需要大量工作来收集、准备和评估文档。这也意味着更新外部知识集合可以改善 Genie 可用的信息，而无需为文档的每次更改重新训练模型。

数据Uber 的 RAG 管道使用内部嵌入模型（可能基于 BERT 变体），检索 top-k 段落（k 通常 5-10），再经 LLM 生成答案。据其博客，答案准确率约 80%，人工兜底率约 20%。

Genie 如何获取所需知识

准备知识与回答问题分开进行。在 Genie 能够检索有用信息之前，Uber 必须将其分散的文档转化为可搜索的集合。这种准备通过 ETL 管道完成。

延伸ETL 是数据工程经典流程，在 RAG 中演变为“文档加载→分块→嵌入→向量存储”。常用工具：LangChain 的 DocumentLoader、Unstructured、LlamaIndex 的 IngestionPipeline。

ETL 代表提取（extract）、转换（transform）和加载（load）。

- 提取意味着从内部来源获取内容。

Genie is Uber’s internal AI assistant for answering engineering support questions. It finds relevant information in Uber’s documentation, uses a language model to explain that information, and gives engineers a way to continue the conversation or ask a human for help.

The Problem Genie Is Trying to Solve

The problem starts with how engineering teams support one another.

In a large software-driven company, many teams build platforms that other developers use to perform their jobs. A platform might provide tools for various activities such as software deployment, data processing, or working with ML workflows. Developers from other teams making use of these platforms inevitably need help with configuration, errors, or understanding unfamiliar features.

In a typical setup, the team responsible for managing a platform assigns an engineer to handle incoming issues during a particular period. This responsibility is commonly about providing on-call support. Although on-call work can definitely involve urgent production incidents, the problem Genie is trying to solve is geared towards everyday tech support that developers on platform teams provide through Slack channels.

For example, a developer from a product team might ask how to configure a platform feature. The on-call engineer reads the question and requests more details. The person asking responds, then waits again. Eventually, the on-call engineer identifies the relevant documentation or explains the required steps. Even a relatively straightforward question can involve several rounds of back-and-forth conversation and waiting.

Uber reports that its internal support channels received approximately 45K questions each month. At that volume, repeated questions consume considerable engineering time that the platform teams could utilize for better stuff.

Much of the information needed to answer such questions already exists somewhere. However, it is often scattered across Engwiki, which is Uber’s internal engineering wiki, or its internal Stack Overflow, and other documents. Finding the right information becomes difficult enough that it is much easier to ask another person.

Genie is designed to make that existing knowledge available through the Slack channels where developers already ask for help.

What does Genie do?

Genie combines finding information with explaining it.

A large language model (LLM) can produce readable explanations and answer technical questions. However, its general training doesn’t automatically give it knowledge of Uber’s private platforms, internal procedures, or documentation.

Therefore, Uber needed a way to connect a language model to its internal knowledge base. Two approaches help with this: fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).

Fine-tuning means further training an existing model using examples selected for a particular purpose. Uber explains that this approach would require a collection of high-quality, varied training examples, along with sufficient computing resources to update the base model as new examples come up.

On the other hand, RAG supplies relevant information when a question is asked. The system first searches a knowledge collection, retrieves useful passages, and then gives those passages to the LLM along with the question. The model uses all this information to generate its answer.

For example, suppose a developer asks how to change a setting in an internal platform. A RAG system searches the platform’s documentation, finds the passage explaining that setting, and gives it to the model. The model turns the retrieved information into a response appropriate to the question.

Uber chose RAG for Genie because it could begin with existing documentation and be ready for launch sooner without the need to first assemble a fine-tuning dataset. This still needed a lot of work to collect, prepare, and evaluate the documentation. It also meant that updating the external knowledge collection could improve the information available to Genie without retraining the model for every change in documentation.

How Genie Gains the Required Knowledge

Preparing knowledge happens separately from answering questions. Before Genie can retrieve useful information, Uber must turn its scattered documents into a searchable collection. This preparation happens using an ETL pipeline.

ETL stands for extract, transform, and load.

- Extraction means fetching content from internal sources.

在事件视界之内：为什么每个人都应该认真对待 AI 的进展

大家好，感谢邀请我。能在这里是极大的荣幸。

首先，我应该列出我的相关资历：我是一个人类。我只有一个。我花了将近 40 年才积累起发表这场演讲的经验。我稍后会回到这些资历。

延伸“我是一个人类”的修辞呼应了图灵测试式的身份焦虑；2025 年后多场学术会议已要求作者声明 LLM 使用情况，人类身份正从默认变为需声明项。

我的整个职业生涯都围绕着寻找那些似乎对世界未来重要的信息，然后告诉世界这些信息。二十年前，我以记者身份起步，专注于数据中心。通过那份工作，我对 AI 如此着迷，以至于 2016 年我离开新闻业去 OpenAI 工作，几年后又联合创办了 Anthropic。从那以后，我一直试图说服世界：有一天，AI 系统可能会变得如此强大，以至于它们能改变国家安全、改变地球的经济轨迹，或许最终获得构建自身的能力。

背景Clark 2016 年加入 OpenAI 时该机构仅有约 50 人；他 2021 年参与创立 Anthropic，该公司以“宪法 AI”和安全研究为旗号，2025 年估值已超 600 亿美元。

好吧，那个时刻已经到来。AI 现在正在改变国家安全，正在改变世界的经济轨迹——正如我们在这里的许多场次中听到的那样——并且正在为自身的发展做出贡献。

当然，你们中的许多人都知道这一点，你们不得不把 AI 作为塑造你们世界和工作的众多不稳定力量之一来追踪。

我今天在这里要解释的是，AI 正走在带来比我们预期更多变化的道路上，我们应该相应地做好准备。我将解释原因。

多年来，研究人员和科幻作家一直预测这样一个未来：AI 系统大幅加快整体科学和技术进步的节奏，使事物发展得如此之快，以至于人类难以跟上。

他们称之为奇点。我相信，今年我们已经获得了令人信服的证据，表明它已经开始。

数据2025 年 METR 研究显示，AI 能独立完成的任务时长每约 7 个月翻一番；若趋势延续，2028 年前后可达人类数天工作量，这是“奇点开始”最常被引用的量化依据。

作家 Vernor Vinge 在 1993 年就预言了这一点。“当超越人类的智能驱动进步时，那种进步将会快得多。事实上，似乎没有理由认为进步本身不会涉及创造更多智能实体——在更短的时间尺度上，”他写道，并将奇点描述为“一个我们的模型必须被丢弃、新现实主宰的点”。

背景Vinge 1993 年论文《The Coming Technological Singularity》提出“事件视界”隐喻，比 Ray Kurzweil 2005 年《奇点临近》早 12 年，是技术奇点概念最常被引的学术源头。

奇点就在这里。其影响触及生活的每一个方面。但最重要的影响很简单：我们应该预期，在未来几年，不仅是 AI，整个科学的进步节奏都会加快。这将令人困惑和迷失方向，并将带来巨大的财富和潜在巨大的危险。

质疑“整个科学加速”缺乏历史类比：20 世纪物理学革命也曾在 30 年内重塑世界，但并未出现可测的“进步速率”跃升；科学计量学中论文引用延迟反而在变长。

今天，我将集中讨论第一点：AI 快速进步的潜在好处，以及我们需要做什么来共同分享这些好处。

人类将从 AI 中受益的许多方式都来自科学。我相信，AI 驱动的科学发明将改变全球经济的走向，改变各国规划和建设自身基础设施的方式，使我们所有人更健康、能够花更多时间与亲人在一起，并且——同时——促使我们所有人就其持续发展应做的选择提出深刻的哲学问题。

我将展开我的主张：在过去的半个世纪里，大多数国家都在应对研究生产率下降的问题，同时研究人员数量快速增长——想法和发明似乎越来越难找到。

数据Bloom 等 2020 年论文《Are Ideas Getting Harder to Find?》测算：美国研发人员数量自 1930 年代增长 20 余倍，但全要素生产率年增速从约 2% 降至 0.5%，即“研究生产率”下降约 40 倍。

但一些重要的事情已经改变：我们现在构建的 AI 系统已经能够增强、扩展和自动化人类劳动的某些方面。这在软件开发中最为明显，AI 系统现在比世界上绝大多数软件工程师更有能力和效率。

质疑“比绝大多数软件工程师更有能力和效率”是演讲者的强断言，但 2025 年 SWE-bench 上顶级模型解决率约 70%，且多在限定仓库与测试下；真实工程中的需求模糊与跨团队协作仍未覆盖。

因此，软件开发领域正在改变——现在一个人能够为自己能想象的任何东西编写软件，创造出以前需要整个团队才能完成的东西。

Import AI 477: In which I tell the IMF the Singularity has begun

by Jack Clark

Welcome to Import AI, a newsletter about AI research. Import AI runs on arXiv, cappuccinos, and feedback from readers. If you’d like to support this, please subscribe.

Here’s a speech I gave on Saturday the 17th of October at the IMF meeting in Bangkok. This speech is inspired by the megatrend readers have seen me cover this year of AI beginning to operate at the frontier of human science, sometimes autonomously. While we all may quibble over the details, I think it’s evident we’re in the early stages of the singularity, and I wanted to use my privileged position of giving the Per Jacobsson lecture to try and tell the assembled audience of economists, bankers, and policymakers, that they should expect significant, rapid change in the future. In this speech, I focused primarily on how humanity can share in the benefits of AI if we exercise proper foresight and restraint, and I’m going to be expanding on the stakes of the singularity and the risks that we’ll all face in coming issues.

As I said a few times in the speech – some of which was ad libbed and not captured here – I take my own responsibility here extremely seriously. I find myself both a narrator of what is happening in AI, a principle agent behind what is happening in AI, and a translator that goes between many communities. In all of this, I am seeking to be truthful and to be clear about my own culpability. This is a forever work-in-progress.

Thank you all for reading. Speeches like this draw heavily from the newsletter, and the newsletter is my attempt to provide week-by-week information to all of you. I appreciate your readership.

Inside the Event Horizon: Why Everyone Should Take AI Progress Seriously

Hello, thank you for having me. It is a great privilege to be here.

To start, I should list my relevant credentials: I am a human being. There is one of me. It has taken me almost 40 years to build the experience to give this speech. I will come back to those credentials later.

My whole career has been defined by finding information that seemed important to the future of the world, then telling the world about that information.Two decades ago, I started my career as a journalist, focusing on data centers. Through that work, I became so obsessed with AI that in 2016 I left journalism to work at OpenAI, then co-founded Anthropic a few years later. Ever since, I’ve been trying to convince the world that one day, AI systems might become so powerful that they could alter national security, change the economic trajectory of the planet, and perhaps eventually gain the ability to build themselves.

Well, the moment has arrived. AI is now changing national security, it is altering the economic trajectory of the world – as we’ve heard in so many sessions here – and is contributing to its own development.

Of course, many of you know this, and you are having to track AI as one of a multitude of destabilizing forces that is shaping your world and your work.

What I am here to explain today is that AI is on a course to bring about even more change than what we are expecting and we should prepare accordingly. And I’m going to explain why.

For many years, researchers and science fiction authors have forecast a future in which AI systems drastically increase the pace of overall scientific and technical progress, making things move so quickly that it is hard for humans to keep up.

They call this the singularity. I believe that this year we have gained convincing evidence that it has now begun.

The writer Vernor Vinge forecast this in 1993. “When greater-than-human intelligence drives progress, that progress will be much more rapid. In fact, there seems no reason why progress itself would not involve the creation of still more intelligent entities — on a still-shorter time scale,” he wrote, describing the singularity as “a point where our models must be discarded and a new reality rules.”

The singularity is here. The implications reach across every aspect of life. But the most important implication is a simple one: we should expect that in the coming years, the whole pace of progress of not just AI, but science as a whole, will speed up. This will feel confusing and disorienting, and it will bring with it great wealth and potentially great peril.

Today, I’m going to concentrate on the first point: the potential benefits of rapid AI progress, and what we need to do to share in these benefits together.

So many of the ways that humanity will benefit from AI come from science. I believe that AI-driven scientific invention will change the course of the global economy, alter the ways countries plan and build their own infrastructure, make us all healthier and able to spend more time with our loved ones, and –– at the same time –– cause us all to ask deep philosophical questions about choices we should make about its continued development.

I’ll develop my claim: For the last half century, most countries have dealt with falling research productivity while researcher headcount grows rapidly – ideas and inventions appear to be getting harder to find.

But something important has changed: we’ve now built AI systems which have been able to augment, extend, and automate aspects of human labor. This has most obviously happened in software development, where AI systems are now more capable and efficient than the vast majority of the world’s software engineers.

As a consequence, the field of software development is changing – A single person is now able to write software for anything they can imagine, creating things that previously would have taken entire teams of humans.

We’ve seen concrete evidence of this inside Anthropic, where our researchers now produce almost 10 times the amount of code than they did for the first four years of the company’s life.
